How much do I need to replace my salary?

Formula: target × 12 ÷ (yield × (1 − withholding tax)). Naira yields look high partly because inflation is high, so raise your target over time. A dividend that grows slower than inflation still loses buying power.

How to use this to replace a 9–5

  1. Accept that dividends are lumpy. Nigerian payouts bunch in April–May (bank and blue-chip finals) and July–November (interims). No major NGX company pays in January.
  2. Pay yourself a salary from a dividend reserve. Send every dividend into one savings or money-market account, then pay yourself the same amount each month (your annual total ÷ 12). The planner's "pay yourself" figure is this number.
  3. Fill the thin months on purpose. The listed infrastructure funds (NIDF quarterly, Coronation twice a year) and REITs qualify you in January and pay in February. Vitafoam pays in March, Julius Berger in June, and Airtel Africa and Aradel in December.
  4. Diversify by sector and payer. Banks dominate Nigerian dividends, so cap any one sector. If the regulator blocks bank dividends (as happened with Access and UBA), you lose several months at once.
  5. Watch the cut-off, not the payment date. You must be on the register at the close of business on the qualification date. Shares bought after that trade "ex-dividend" and the payout goes to the seller.
  6. Register for e-dividend with each registrar so payments land in your bank account, and claim any unpaid old dividends.
  7. Reinvest while you're still working. Until you retire, reinvesting dividends grows the income much faster than the payouts alone.
