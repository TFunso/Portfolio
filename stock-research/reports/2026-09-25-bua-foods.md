- BUA Foods Plc · NGX: BUAFOODS · Nigeria · Consumer staples / packaged foods (sugar, flour, pasta, rice)
- Price ~₦715 · 18bn shares · Market cap ~₦12.9T (~$9.7bn), the most valuable food company on NGX · Currency: NGN
- Controlled by Abdul Samad Rabiu (direct and via BUA entities, ~92.6%).

## 2. Business quality — Good (18/25)

- **Fact:** FY2025 revenue ₦1.77T (+16%); operating profit ₦656.6bn; net margin ~38% (far above NASCON ~24%, Nestlé Nigeria ~10%, Cadbury Nigeria ~4%).
- **Opinion:** Staple foods will always sell in a country of 200m+ people, and BUA's scale and integration (own sugar estate and refining, flour milling, pasta, rice) give it a cost edge. But these are commodity products: pricing follows inflation and FX, and competitors (Dangote Sugar, Flour Mills, Olam and imports) can win share when prices fall. Margins this high rarely last in food processing.

## 4. Management — 5/10

- Strong execution: margin expansion, debt reduction, fast pivot to pasta as sugar and flour prices fell.
- **Penalties:**
  - **Extreme ownership concentration:** ~92.6% held by the founder and related entities; free float ~4.7% (~849m shares). Minority shareholders have almost no influence, and the stock is thinly traded.
  - **Payout ~97%** (₦28 dividend on ₦28.80 EPS; ₦504bn total, of which ~$323m goes to the controlling shareholder). Generous for minorities too, but leaves little retained capital and suggests dividends are set to suit the owner.
  - Related-party dealings with the wider BUA Group: detail not verified.

## 6. Growth — 8/15

- **Drivers:** pasta (+70%), rice, capacity additions, possible volume recovery as inflation falls and real incomes improve.
- **Headwinds:** falling food prices shrink revenue (H1 −16%); profit growth now relies on cost cuts and lower finance costs, which have limits. With a ~100% payout, growth capital must come from debt or cash flow.
- Consensus target ~₦904 (+26%), but targets have lagged the stock's fall (−19% over 90 days).

## 7. Risk — 4/10 · **High**

1. **Margin mean-reversion** at a premium multiple (biggest risk).
2. **Government intervention** on food prices or import duties. Nigeria allowed duty-free food imports in 2024, which hurt local processors.
3. **Imported inputs** (wheat) and FX exposure for flour and pasta.
4. **Illiquidity and governance:** ~4.7% free float, 92.6% controlling owner.
5. **Naira risk** for dollar investors.

## 8. Emerging-market test — Country risk 65/100 (higher = riskier)

Food prices are politically sensitive in Nigeria (import waivers, price pressure). Wheat is imported, so naira weakness raises costs. Easing inflation is currently cutting BUA's selling prices. Dividend repatriation depends on FX liquidity. High country risk means a high required return (~20%+ in naira), which a ~4.5% earnings yield does not meet.

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Net margin falls from ~38% toward the ~20–25% typical of good food processors: EPS drops ~30–40%.
2. Government reopens duty-free food imports or leans on staple prices.
3. Naira weakens, raising wheat costs while selling prices lag.
4. Dividend cut from a ~100% payout triggers selling in a thin market.
5. The multiple compresses from ~22x toward the Nigerian consumer average (~15x).

What investors may overlook: revenue has been shrinking for two quarters, and 2026 profit growth comes from cost cuts and lower interest costs, not from selling more.

## If I had $10,000

**0% at ₦715.** Buy ~3% below ~₦550 (~17x earnings, ~5% yield), or if H2 2026 shows revenue stabilising with margins intact. For Nigerian exposure today, GTCO (Buy) offers a far higher yield at a far lower multiple.

## Investment committee decision — **Watchlist**

BUA Foods is a genuinely strong operator: integrated, low-debt, and the most profitable food company in Nigeria. But at ~22–25x earnings and ~20x book, the price assumes record margins last while revenue is falling, and ~95% of the shares sit with one owner. Our weighted value (~₦631) is below today's price, and the bear case is twice as large as the bull case. Protect capital first: watch, and buy below ~₦550.
