## 2. Business quality — Excellent (21/25)

- **Fact:** FY2026 revenue $36.4bn (+8.4% reported, +5.8% organic), its fastest growth in 10 years. Portfolios: Cardiovascular (pacemakers, defibrillators, heart ablation, heart valves), Neuroscience, Medical Surgical (including the Hugo surgical robot) and Diabetes (being separated as MiniMed).
- **Opinion:** Hospitals rely on Medtronic devices across hundreds of procedures; implanted devices and disposables recur, and switching costs (surgeon training, hospital contracts) are high. Weakness: in the hottest markets it has often been a follower. Boston Scientific's Farapulse led pulsed field ablation and Intuitive dominates robotic surgery; Hugo was only cleared in the US in December 2025.

## 4. Management — 7/10

- CEO Geoff Martha. Portfolio clean-up: Diabetes IPO'd as **MiniMed** (March 2026, Medtronic kept ~90%); split-off exchange offer launched 14 Sep 2026 to hand out at least 80.1% tax-free.
- Growth bets are paying off: pulsed field ablation (Affera Sphere-9, PulseSelect) drove cardiac ablation +88% in Q1; Hugo robot cleared December 2025, more filings submitted June 2026.
- Dividend raised for the **49th consecutive year** (to $0.72/quarter).
- **Penalties:** FY2026 EPS growth was only 0.7%. Bought back 10m shares in FY2026 at an average $93.25, above today's price. Late to both the pulsed field ablation and robotic surgery markets.

## 6. Growth — 11/15

- **Drivers:** pulsed field ablation (the biggest growth engine in heart rhythm), cardiac rhythm management +15%, Hugo robot entering the US, renal denervation (hypertension), ageing populations.
- **Caution:** Q1's +13.7% is well above the full-year guide of ~7.5%, which implies the rest of the year slows to roughly 5–6%. Treat Q1 as a peak, not a run-rate.
- Analyst consensus: Buy, average target ~$105 (+17%).

## 7. Risk — 7/10 · **Low–Medium**

1. **Competition** in pulsed field ablation (Boston Scientific, J&J, Abbott) and robotics (Intuitive).
2. **Growth normalisation** after a strong Q1; a miss would hurt.
3. **Debt** of ~$28bn (manageable, ~2.6x EBITDA).
4. **MiniMed separation:** exchange-offer mechanics and share flows can pressure the price near completion.
5. **Tariffs, pricing and product recalls** (usual medtech risks).

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Q1 proves a one-off; growth reverts to the 4–5% of past years and the market re-labels it "ex-growth" (~12x).
2. Boston Scientific and J&J out-compete it in pulsed field ablation, its main growth engine.
3. Hugo fails to win share from Intuitive, wasting years of R&D.
4. A major product recall (medtech's recurring risk).
5. Debt-funded M&A to buy growth after MiniMed leaves.

What investors may overlook: FY2026 EPS barely grew (+0.7%), so the "re-acceleration" story rests on one strong quarter plus guidance.

## Investment committee decision — **Buy**

Medtronic offers large-cap medtech quality at a below-peer price: ~15x earnings, a 3.2% yield covered 1.5x by free cash flow, and a growth engine (pulsed field ablation) that is finally working. The expected return (~15% over a year, ~10% a year over five) clears our hurdle with upside larger than downside. It stops short of Strong Buy because the acceleration rests on one standout quarter, debt is meaningful, and the MiniMed separation adds near-term noise. Buy a core position now; add on weakness.
