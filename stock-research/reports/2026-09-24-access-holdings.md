## 2. Business quality — Good (17/25)

- **Fact:** One of Nigeria's largest banks by assets (₦51.6T FY2025; ₦53.4T Mar 2026). Deposits ₦34.6T (+53%). Fees and commissions +41% in FY2025.
- **Opinion:** Banking demand in 10 years is secure; retail and payments franchise gives some stickiness. The moat is scale, not pricing power. Funding costs averaged ~61% of interest income over three years vs ~33% for Tier-1 peers (CardinalStone), which points to a weaker deposit franchise than GTCO or Zenith. Loans are only ~25% of assets and securities (₦16.3T) exceed loans (₦13.3T).

**Recession survival:** Probably yes (adequate capital, early ₦500bn recapitalisation), but earnings would drop sharply given reliance on FX and securities income.

## 4. Management — 5/10

- Chairman Aigboje Aig-Imoukhuede returned March 2024 after Herbert Wigwe's death; Innocent Ike Group CEO since August 2025. Chairman says the acquisition phase is over and focus is shareholder returns.
- **Penalties:** repeated late results needing CBN sign-off; regulatory breaches (FHC guideline in H1 2025, then BOFIA §19(8)(c): foreign-subsidiary investment 19.3% of shareholders' funds vs 10% cap); heavy dilution (rights issue at ₦19.75, 2026 placement at ₦20.25, both far below ~₦80 book value per share; share count up ~50%); had to disclaim an unauthorised FY2025 results summary.

## 7. Risk — 4/10 · **High**

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. FX gains reverse (₦176bn in one quarter can swing the other way).
2. Rate cuts compress yields on a securities book larger than the loan book.
3. Impairments keep doubling as acquired portfolios in new markets deteriorate.
4. CBN keeps blocking dividends or forces subsidiary sales at poor prices.
5. Another capital raise below book value.
6. Nigerian sovereign or FX stress returns.

What investors miss: the ~2x P/E flatters because reported profit includes non-repeatable items, and the discount could persist indefinitely without dividends.

## Investment committee decision — **Hold**

The numbers say cheap: 2.2x earnings, 0.4x book, weighted value ~₦38. But a large share of profit comes from unhedged FX gains and bond income, impairments are accelerating, the regulator has blocked dividends twice in a year, management has raised equity at about a quarter of book, and half-year results await CBN approval. When evidence is mixed, take the conservative reading. Catalysts to watch: dividend resumption and a clean H1 2026. Until then, token position only; serious buying below ₦25.
