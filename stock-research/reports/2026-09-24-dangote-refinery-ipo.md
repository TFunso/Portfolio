## 1. The company and the offer

- Dangote Petroleum Refinery & Petrochemicals FZE, Lekki Free Zone, Lagos. Oil refining and petrochemicals.
- **Offer:** 4.1bn new shares at ₦525, raising ₦2.15T (~$1.6bn), plus up to 30% more on demand. **Only 3.3% of the company.** Money goes to the company for expansion; the founder is not selling.
- **Dates:** 14 Sep – 13 Oct 2026; NGX listing expected November 2026.
- **Valuation:** 124.23bn shares × ₦525 = **₦65.22T (~$48bn)**.
- **Minimum:** 10 shares (₦5,250).
- **Ownership after IPO (opinion piece, not prospectus):** Dangote ~84%, NNPC ~6.6%.
- **July 2026 private placement:** 3bn shares at $0.35 (~₦466–478) to institutions, 365-day lock-up. **IPO buyers pay ~10% more.**

## 2. Business quality — Good to Excellent (19/25)

- **Fact:** Africa's largest refinery (650k bpd as built, ~700k after upgrade). Build cost reported near $20bn. Became Europe's largest jet-fuel supplier during Middle East disruption (~80k bpd).
- **Opinion:** Near-monopoly on local production, extremely hard to replicate. But refining is a commodity business with no pricing power, and it depends on NNPC for naira-priced crude; shortfalls force costlier dollar imports.

## 3. Financial strength — 11/20

| Period | Result |
|---|---|
| FY2024 | Net loss ~$1.51bn |
| FY2025 | Net loss $476m; repeated major-unit shutdowns late 2025–early 2026 |
| H1 2026 | Revenue $13.91bn (+121%), EBITDA $2.60bn (18.7%), **PAT $1.82bn** |
| Gross refining margin ($/bbl) | $10.70 (2024) → $13.70 (2025) → **$24.50 (H1 2026)** |
| Debt | $6.24bn (Dec 2025) → **$5.67bn** (Jun 2026); $3.99bn parent loan repaid |

**Earnings-quality flags:**
1. **War windfall.** Margins nearly doubled because the Iran war took Middle East fuel supply offline.
2. **Related-party hedge.** A $410m hedging loss with external banks was offset by a derivative with Dangote Industries (the parent) — ~23% of H1 profit.
3. **Temporary tax holiday.** Free-zone status keeps tax near zero; domestic-sales profits may become taxable from 1 Jan 2028, and the benefit depends on ≤25% of sales going to the Nigerian market.

## 4. Management — 5/10

Positives: Aliko Dangote built an asset many said couldn't be built; no insider selling in the IPO. Negatives: controlled company (~84%); large related-party dealings; FZE structure means lighter disclosure and changeable tax regime; 3.3% float; construction overruns and unit outages; dollar-dividend promise **not verified as formal prospectus policy**.

## 5. Valuation — Overvalued (6/20)

| Metric | At ₦525 |
|---|---|
| Market cap | ~$48bn |
| EV/EBITDA (H1 annualised) | ~9.5x |
| P/E (H1 annualised) | ~13x |
| Peer EV/EBITDA | Valero 8.5x, Marathon 8.2x, industry median 7.5x |
| Dividend yield | 0% (no history) |

**Key calculation:** EV ≈ $47.8bn + $5.67bn debt = ~$53.5bn (cash unknown). At the peer ~8.5x, that requires **~$6.3bn EBITDA every year** — more than the war-peak run-rate of $5.2bn. At 2025-level margins (~$14/bbl), EBITDA is plausibly $2.5–3.5bn/yr (estimate).

## 6. Growth — 11/15

Upside: doubling to 1.4m bpd ($14.3bn, by 2028–29); petrochemicals (~$6.4bn planned polypropylene/fertiliser exports); management expects tight global fuel markets post-war. Catch: IPO funds only ~11% of the expansion; the rest is debt and retained earnings, which limits dividends.

## 7. Risk — 3/10 · **High**

## 8. Emerging-market test — Country risk 60/100 (higher = riskier)

Offsetting: revenue is largely dollar-linked (exports, import-parity pricing); dividends promised in USD. Against: shares priced in naira; high government-intervention risk (fuel pricing politics, NNPC crude allocation, government-set tax status); dividend payments depend on Nigeria's FX regime.

## 10. Dividends — Quality 25/100

No history. USD-dividend promise not confirmed as formal policy. $14.3bn capex and deleveraging compete for cash. High probability dividends disappoint in years 1–3 (opinion).

## 11. Insiders — Mixed

Aligned: founder not selling; ~84% insider ownership. Not aligned: minority holders have little influence; two-way related-party flows. **Overhang:** 3bn placement shares (≈ size of the IPO) unlock around July 2027.

## 12. Fair value

Method: normalised EBITDA × multiple − $5.67bn debt, ÷ 124.23bn shares, at ₦1,332/$.

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value | vs ₦525 | Probability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | EBITDA $2.3bn, 7.5x | ₦124 | −76% | 25% |
| Base | EBITDA $3.5bn, 10x | ₦314 | −40% | 50% |
| Bull | EBITDA $5.2bn, 12x | ₦608 | +16% | 25% |

**Probability-weighted value: ~₦340 (≈ −35%).** 1-year: fundamentally negative, though a listing pop is possible given the tiny float (a trade, not an investment). 3-year: ~0–5%/yr naira (estimate). 5-year: ~5–10%/yr if expansion succeeds at ~$15/bbl margins — below an 8% hurdle after naira risk.

Downside exceeds upside → cannot be rated Buy.

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Margins normalise from $24.50 to $11–14/bbl; profits roughly halve; ~13x P/E becomes ~25–30x.
2. NNPC naira-crude shortfalls force dollar imports.
3. Free-zone tax benefits shrink from 2028, or domestic sales exceed 25%.
4. Expansion repeats the first build's overruns → more debt or dilution.
5. Placement unlock (July 2027) and any US listing add supply.
6. Political pressure on fuel prices, regulator disputes, FX rules on USD dividends.

## If I had $10,000

**0% as an investment.** At most 1% ($100) as a speculative listing trade, sold on a pop. Better: wait for one full audited year (FY2026), post-war margin data, clarity on the 2027 unlock; buy below ~₦330.

## Investment committee decision — **Watchlist**

Probably Nigeria's most important industrial asset — which is exactly why discipline matters. At ₦525 you pay a premium to proven global refiners for one profitable half-year that was boosted by a war, helped by a parent-company hedge, and taxed at a rate that starts eroding in 2028. Weighted value ~₦340; the bear case is far worse than the bull case is good. When downside exceeds upside, we don't buy. Revisit after listing and FY2026 audited results.
