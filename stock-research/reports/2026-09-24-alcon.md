The world's largest eye-care company, down ~16% in a year after a revenue miss in Q1 and a $402m write-off. Two things keep this from a Strong Buy: weak returns on its large acquired asset base, and a string of capital-allocation misses.

## 1. Company

- Alcon Inc. · NYSE and SIX Swiss Exchange: ALC · Switzerland (HQ Geneva/Fort Worth) · Healthcare / medical devices, eye care

- Spun off from Novartis in 2019.

## 2. Business quality — Excellent (21/25)

- **Fact:** 2025 sales $10.3bn (+5%). Two segments: **Surgical** (cataract and retina equipment, intraocular lenses (IOLs), consumables; Q2 2026 $1.57bn) and **Vision Care** (contact lenses, eye drops; Q2 2026 $1.21bn).
- **Opinion:** Cataract surgery demand grows with an ageing world population, and most revenue is recurring (lenses, surgical consumables, contact lenses, eye drops). Surgeons trained on Alcon equipment tend to stay with it, and the new Unity platform refresh (equipment +26% in Q2) locks in the next cycle. Weakness: premium IOL share is slipping outside the US (Japan, parts of Asia, China budget pressure), and contact lenses face strong competition from Cooper and J&J.

**Weak spot (my calculation):** core ROE is only ~7% (core EPS × shares ÷ equity) and GAAP ROE ~4%. The balance sheet carries the goodwill and intangibles inherited from Novartis and acquisitions, so returns on capital are mediocre even though margins are healthy.

**Recession survival:** Yes. Cataract surgery is largely non-discretionary, net debt is under 2x core operating income, and debt is long-dated and fixed-rate.

## 4. Management — 6/10

- Positives: Unity equipment launch executing well; Tryptyr (dry eye) and Systane driving ocular health (+13%); ~$680m of buybacks in 2025 (~1.7% of shares); guidance raised mid-year.
- **Penalties:**
  - **$402m write-off (Q2 2026)** of the PowerVision lens programmes bought in 2019, after trial results stayed unpredictable. Seven years of spend with nothing to show.
  - **STAAR Surgical deal failed** (Jan 2026): STAAR shareholders rejected Alcon's $1.5bn offer even after it was raised.
  - Q1 2026 revenue miss and guidance cut drew a JPMorgan downgrade ("another soft quarter"). The company now leans on a heavy Q4 to hit targets.
- Dividend held flat at CHF 0.28/share for 2025 and 2026.

## 6. Growth — 11/15

- **Drivers:** Unity equipment upgrade cycle, ageing population (cataract volumes), ocular health and dry eye (Tryptyr), premium contact lenses, margin expansion (core EPS growing ~2x as fast as sales).
- **Headwinds:** IOL share losses outside the US, China pricing pressure, tariffs ($91m cost in 2025; now guided to $40–90m/yr after an expected ~$60m US refund), and a back-end-loaded 2026.
- Analyst consensus: Buy, average target ~$85 (+30%).

## 7. Risk — 7/10 · **Low–Medium**

1. **Execution/credibility:** another revenue miss would hit a stock that already fell ~11% in one day on Q1.
2. **IOL competition** and share loss outside the US.
3. **Capital allocation:** history of acquisitions that disappoint; the need to buy growth remains.
4. **Tariffs and trade** (US–Swiss).
5. **Currency:** reports in USD, but a large share of sales is non-US.

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Another revenue miss plus guidance cut: the market already punished Q1 by ~11% in one day.
2. The Unity cycle peaks early and equipment growth reverses.
3. Premium IOL share losses spread to the US.
4. A large, expensive acquisition to buy growth (after STAAR failed) that repeats the PowerVision outcome.
5. Multiple falls to peer lows (~14–15x) on "ex-growth" fears: with flat earnings, that alone is ~−20–25%.

What investors may overlook: headline core EPS excludes amortisation and write-offs that are real costs of past deals, and returns on the $22bn of equity are only mid-single-digit.

## Investment committee decision — **Buy (starter position)**

Alcon is a durable, mostly recurring eye-care franchise priced at ~19x earnings that are guided to grow 12–15% this year. The share price already reflects a year of disappointments: a Q1 miss, a failed acquisition and a $402m write-off. That leaves a reasonable ~10% expected return with upside (+41%) larger than downside (−22%). It is not a Strong Buy: returns on capital are weak, management's deal record is poor, and the 2026 plan depends on a strong fourth quarter. Buy a starter position now and add on weakness or proof of delivery.
