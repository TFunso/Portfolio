Nigeria's fastest-improving mid-sized bank: H1 2026 profit +50%, ROE ~40%, capital ratio ~28%, at only ~5x earnings. The catch: profit is being driven by very fast loan growth (+22% in six months) and a jump in trading gains, just as interest rates start to fall. Cheap, but riskier and lower-quality than GTCO.

## 1. Company

- Wema Bank Plc · NGX: WEMABANK · Nigeria · Financials / commercial banking (national licence); pioneer of ALAT, Nigeria's first fully digital bank
- Price ~₦30 · ~40.12bn shares · Market cap ~₦1.2T (~$0.9bn) · Currency: NGN

## 2. Business quality — Average to Good (15/25)

- **Fact:** Total assets ₦5.76T (June 2026, +13.5% in six months); deposits ₦3.45T; loans ₦2.12T.
- **Opinion:** A Tier-2 bank with a real digital edge (ALAT drives about a fifth of fee income). But it lacks the scale, low-cost deposit base and pricing power of GTCO or Zenith, operates on a national (not international) licence, and fee income fell 20% in H1. Its recent surge comes more from balance-sheet growth than from a durable moat.

## 4. Management — 7/10

- Completed recapitalisation about six months early (₦40bn first tranche, ₦150bn rights issue, ₦50bn special placement).
- Strong execution: cost-to-income down, NPLs down, profit more than doubled in 2025.
- ALAT shows genuine innovation.
- **Penalties:** repeated capital raises have diluted existing shareholders (current ~40.12bn shares; issue prices not verified). Much of the profit jump comes from trading gains and loan growth rather than fees.

## 5. Valuation — Undervalued (16/20)

| Multiple | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E (trailing 12 months, ~₦5.94 EPS) | ~5.1x |

| Dividend yield | ~4.1% (₦1.25 for FY2025; payout ~26%) |
| Earnings yield | ~20% |

Nigerian bank median: ~5.65x earnings, ~1.0x book. Wema is at the median P/E but earns a far higher ROE (~40%). Paying 1.7x book for 40% ROE is cheap *if* that ROE is sustainable.

## 6. Growth — 11/15

- **Drivers:** loan book +22% in six months, deposits growing, digital customers (ALAT), fresh capital to lend.
- **Headwinds:** the CBN cut its policy rate to 23% (Sept 2026), which will squeeze loan yields; trading gains of this size rarely repeat; fee income is falling.
- The stock is already up ~45% this year and near its 52-week high (₦36).

## 7. Risk — 4/10 · **High**

1. **Credit risk from rapid loan growth** heading into a slowing, rate-cutting cycle.
2. **Earnings quality:** trading gains up 657%, fees down 20%.
3. **Naira and sovereign risk.**
4. **Further dilution** if the bank needs capital to keep growing.
5. **Scale:** a mid-sized bank competing against far larger, better-funded Tier-1 banks.

## 8. Emerging-market test — Country risk 65/100 (higher = riskier)

Same Nigeria backdrop as the other bank reports: inflation ~15% (falling), naira ~₦1,330/$ (stable for now, history of sharp devaluations), CBN controls dividend approvals, cost of equity ~20–24% in naira. Wema's higher loan exposure makes it more sensitive to a macro downturn than liquid Tier-1 banks.

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Loans grown fast in a high-rate period start to sour as the economy slows: impairments rise from a very low base (₦4.3bn in H1).
2. Rate cuts compress loan and securities yields; trading gains disappear.
3. Fee income keeps falling as payment competition (fintechs, Moniepoint, OPay) intensifies.
4. Another capital raise at a discount.
5. A Nigeria-wide selloff (naira, sovereign) hits smaller banks hardest.

What investors may overlook: the stock is up ~45% this year on a profit surge that partly reflects one-off trading gains.

## If I had $10,000

**1–2% ($100–200) now** as a speculative satellite to GTCO. Raise to ~3% below ~₦26 (~1.5x book, ~4x earnings). Keep total Nigeria exposure to ~10–15% of the portfolio. If choosing one Nigerian bank, GTCO first.

## Investment committee decision — **Hold**

Wema is cheap on the numbers (~5x earnings for ~40% ROE) and has executed well: early recapitalisation, falling NPLs, profit up 50%. But the quality of that growth is weaker than it looks: loans up 22% in six months, trading gains up 657% and fees down 20%, all heading into a rate-cutting cycle, in a smaller bank that has repeatedly diluted shareholders. The expected return is attractive, so a small position is reasonable, but it doesn't earn a Buy against GTCO's stronger franchise. Hold; accumulate below ~₦26.
