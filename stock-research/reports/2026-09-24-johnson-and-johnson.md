## 1. Company

- Johnson & Johnson · NYSE: JNJ · USA · Healthcare: pharmaceuticals (Innovative Medicine) and medical devices (MedTech)
- Price $270.41 · all-time high $278.43 (3 Sep 2026) · Market cap ~$649bn · ~2.40bn shares · Currency: USD

## 2. Business quality — Excellent (23/25)

- **Fact:** 2025 sales $94.2bn (+6.0%). Q2 2026 sales $25.3bn (+6.6%); on track for first-ever $100bn+ year. Two segments: Innovative Medicine (oncology, immunology, neuroscience) and MedTech (cardiovascular, surgery, orthopaedics, vision).
- **Opinion:** Demand for cancer, immunology and cardiac treatment is about as durable as it gets. Scale in R&D and commercial reach is a real moat, and the portfolio is diversified enough that no single product sinks it. Weakness: individual drugs lose patents on a clock (Stelara now, Darzalex ~2029), so growth has to be re-bought constantly through R&D and acquisitions.

## 4. Management — 8/10

- CEO Joaquin Duato. Clear strategy: focus on higher-growth pharma and MedTech (Kenvue consumer spin in 2023; orthopaedics separation targeted mid-to-late 2027, with an outright sale for $20bn+ also explored).
- Acquisitions: Abiomed (2022), Shockwave (2024), Intra-Cellular (~$14.5bn, 2025), Halda (2025). Growth products from these deals (Shockwave, Caplyta) are contributing.
- Dividend raised for the **64th consecutive year** (April 2026, +3.1% to $1.34/quarter).
- **Penalty:** three failed attempts to push talc claims through subsidiary bankruptcies (last rejected March 2025) cost years and credibility before the July 2026 settlement.

## 6. Growth — 10/15

**Drivers (Q2 2026 / H1 2026):** Darzalex H1 sales $8.2bn (+20.6%); Carvykti H1 $1.25bn (+55%); Tremfya first $2bn quarter; Rybrevant, Tecvayli, Spravato, Caplyta growing; MedTech +3.6% operational (Shockwave, electrophysiology). Innovative Medicine +6.8% operational despite Stelara falling ~56% from biosimilars.

**Headwinds:** Darzalex (J&J's largest drug) loses US patent protection ~2029; MFN pricing deal and Medicare negotiation pressure US prices; orthopaedics (slow grower, ~$9.2bn sales) is being separated. Guidance implies ~7–8% EPS growth, solid but not exceptional.

## 7. Risk — 7/10 · **Low–Medium**

1. **Valuation compression** (most likely source of loss).
2. **Darzalex patent cliff ~2029** (~11% of 2023 revenue).
3. **US drug pricing:** MFN deal (bought a three-year tariff reprieve) and IRA negotiation.
4. **Talc:** $5.5bn settlement covers existing ovarian claims only (~76,000 suits, needs 95% participation); future claims and mesothelioma claims remain open.
5. **Execution** on acquisitions and the orthopaedics separation.

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Multiple reverts from ~23x toward the ~16x historical range with no change in the business: that alone is ~−30%.
2. Darzalex biosimilars arrive ~2029 and the pipeline doesn't fully replace them.
3. MFN pricing spreads from TrumpRx and Medicaid to commercial prices.
4. Talc settlement fails the 95% participation threshold, or future/mesothelioma claims escalate.
5. An expensive acquisition to fill the cliff disappoints.

What investors may be overlooking: the stock has priced in the good news already (57% in a year, near an all-time high), and a raised-guidance quarter is now expected, not rewarded.

## If I had $10,000

**3–5% ($300–500) now** as a small quality position: it is low-risk, pays in dollars and removes the currency risk the Nigerian holdings carry. Scale toward **5–10% (core holding) below ~$230** (~20x 2026 earnings, ~2.3% yield).

## Investment committee decision — **Hold**

J&J is exactly the kind of business you want to own for decades: ~$20bn a year of free cash flow, diversified growth drivers, a 64-year dividend streak, and dollar-denominated returns. The problem is price, not quality. At ~23x forward earnings after a 57% run, you need everything to go right to earn more than a mid-single-digit return, while a simple reversion to its historical multiple would cost ~30%. Protect capital first: hold if owned, start small if not, and buy seriously below ~$230.
