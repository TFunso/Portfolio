## 1. Company

- CWG Plc (formerly Computer Warehouse Group) · NGX: CWG · Nigeria (operations also in Ghana, Uganda, Cameroon, UAE) · Technology / IT services and infrastructure
- Price ~₦19.40 · ~2.52bn shares · Market cap ~₦49bn (~$37m) · Currency: NGN

## 2. Business quality — Average (13/25)

- **Fact:** Revenue ₦65.6bn in FY2025 (+41%); IT infrastructure services ₦15.5bn in H1 2026 (+142%, now ~35% of revenue). Ghana ~17% of sales.
- **Opinion:** Demand for IT infrastructure, cloud and managed services in Nigerian banks, telcos and government will grow. But much of CWG's revenue is hardware and infrastructure resale and integration, which is low-margin, lumpy and exposed to imported-equipment costs. Customers can switch vendors between projects. It is a small player next to global integrators and OEMs.

**Recession survival:** Likely. It is now nearly debt-free and cash flow has recovered. But a small IT vendor can lose big contracts quickly when clients cut capex, and receivables can balloon again.

## 4. Management — 6/10

- Turnaround executed: revenue scale-up, debt paid down, dividend raised from 39 kobo to 70 kobo.
- Cash collection improved sharply in H1 2026 after a poor 2025.
- **Penalties:** 2025 growth was bought with working capital (negative operating cash flow); possible downward revision between unaudited and audited 2025 profit (see conflict above); limited disclosure and no analyst coverage.

## 6. Growth — 10/15

- **Drivers:** infrastructure services (+142%), cloud and managed services, bank and telco digitisation, regional subsidiaries.
- **Headwinds:** the fastest-growing segment carries lower margins, so profit is lagging revenue (revenue +21%, profit +2%). Imported hardware costs rise if the naira weakens.

## 7. Risk — 3/10 · **High**

1. **Micro-cap and illiquidity:** ~$37m market cap; a few trades can move the price sharply both ways.
2. **Margin compression** from the lower-margin infrastructure mix.
3. **Working-capital swings:** 2025 showed how quickly receivables can absorb cash.
4. **Naira and import costs** for hardware.
5. **Customer and contract concentration** (not disclosed).

## 8. Emerging-market test — Country risk 65/100 (higher = riskier)

Revenue is mostly naira and cedi, while much hardware is priced in dollars. A devaluation squeezes margins before prices can be raised. Ghana (~17% of sales) adds a second currency risk. Dividends are small and paid in naira.

## 9. Competitors

Listed Nigerian tech peers (e.g. Chams, eTranzact) and global integrators: comparable valuation and margin data not available in this session. CWG is among the larger listed Nigerian IT services firms but small globally.

## 10. Dividends — Quality 55/100

1. The rally (+174% in a year) reverses as profit growth stays near zero.
2. Margins keep falling as low-margin infrastructure grows.

## If I had $10,000

**0–1% ($0–100)**, speculative only. Consider up to ~2% below ~₦15 (~7.5x earnings, ~4.7% yield), and only after checking liquidity with a broker. It belongs in the "small bets" part of a portfolio, not the core.

## Investment committee decision — **Hold**

CWG has done the hard part of a turnaround: record revenue, debt paid off, cash flow restored and a bigger dividend. But the market has already rewarded that with a ~174% rise, while H1 2026 profit barely grew and margins are slipping as the business shifts toward lower-margin infrastructure work. At ~10x earnings, a ~$37m illiquid micro-cap with high country risk doesn't offer enough margin of safety. Hold; buy only below ~₦15.
