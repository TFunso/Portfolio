## 1. Company

- Guaranty Trust Holding Company Plc (GTCO) · NGX: GTCO (also cross-listed on the London Stock Exchange) · Nigeria · Financials / banking, with pensions, asset management and payments (HabariPay) subsidiaries
- Price ~₦132 · ~36.55bn shares · Market cap ~₦4.8T (~$3.6bn at ₦1,332/$) · Currency: NGN

## 2. Business quality — Good to Excellent (20/25)

- **Fact:** FY2025 profit before tax ₦1.23T; total assets ₦17.8T (₦18.7T in March 2026); deposits ₦12.87T (+23.8%), ₦13.69T by March 2026.
- **Opinion:** Long-standing reputation as Nigeria's most efficient, best-governed bank, with a low-cost deposit base and strong digital franchise. Customers and deposits keep growing. The limit is the market it is in: banking is commoditised, heavily regulated, and tied to Nigeria's macro cycle.

## 4. Management — 8/10

- Group CEO Segun Agbaje; new chairman Sulieman Barau (ex-CBN deputy governor) as part of a succession plan.
- Completed the ₦500bn bank recapitalisation (rights issue plus a $105m London listing at a ₦70 reference price, July 2025).
- Consistent, transparent reporting and rising dividends.
- **Penalties:** share count rose to ~36.55bn through the recapitalisation (dilution, though regulator-mandated); key-person risk around Agbaje's eventual succession; H1 2026 results delayed awaiting CBN approval (an industry-wide pattern, not GTCO-specific).

## 6. Growth — 9/15

- **Tailwinds:** deposit growth, fee income, excess capital to lend as rates fall, possible re-rating as foreign investors return (LSE listing, stable naira, reserves ~$54.6bn).
- **Headwinds:** the CBN cut its policy rate from 26.5% to 23% (Sept 2026). With loans only ~17% of assets, most income comes from securities, so lower rates cut yields directly. FY2025 profit already fell 15% on new taxes. Q1 loan growth was only 1.3%.

## 7. Risk — 5/10 · **Medium–High**

1. **Naira and sovereign risk** (for a dollar investor, the dominant risk).
2. **Falling interest rates** compressing income on a securities-heavy balance sheet.
3. **Tax and regulatory changes** (new withholding taxes cut 2025 profit; CBN controls dividend approvals).
4. **Asset quality:** group Stage 3 loans ~4–5%.
5. **Leadership succession.**

## 8. Emerging-market test — Country risk 65/100 (higher = riskier)

Inflation ~15% cuts real returns. The naira is currently stable (~₦1,330/$, reserves ~$54.6bn, official/parallel gap ~3.4%), but history shows sharp devaluations. Dividend repatriation has improved but depends on FX liquidity. GTCO's cost of equity is therefore high (~20–24% in naira), which is why even a 25%-ROE bank trades near 1.3x book.

## 10. Dividends — Quality 80/100

₦3.20 (2023) → ₦8.03 (2024) → **₦12.76 (2025)**. Payout ~54% of estimated FY2025 EPS; yield ~9.7%. Dividends kept rising even when profit fell in 2025, backed by a ~40% capital ratio. Risk: the CBN can restrict bank dividends, and falling rates may slow growth. Probability of a cut in the next year: low (~15%, my estimate).

## 13. Bear case — How it falls 30%+

1. Rates fall faster than expected; securities income drops and EPS falls 15–20%.
2. Another tax or levy on banks (as in 2025) cuts profits again.
3. A naira devaluation or sovereign stress hits all Nigerian assets at once.
4. CBN restricts dividends, removing the main support for the share price.
5. Succession stumbles when Agbaje leaves.

What investors may overlook: 2024's record EPS was inflated by FX gains; 2025 profit fell 15%, and the dividend now absorbs over half of earnings.

## If I had $10,000

**5% ($500).** GTCO is the best Nigerian bank to own, so it should be the anchor of any Nigeria allocation, but country risk caps the position. Keep total Nigeria exposure (GTCO plus any other Nigerian stocks) to about 10–15% of the portfolio. Add more below ~₦110 (~1.1x book, ~11.6% yield).

## Investment committee decision — **Buy**

GTCO combines the best qualities available in Nigerian banking: ~25% return on equity, a ~40% capital ratio, very low credit losses and a fast-growing dividend now yielding ~9.7%, all for ~5.6x earnings. Expected return of ~22% a year in naira clears our hurdle even after allowing for naira weakness, and upside is larger than downside. It is not a Strong Buy because the business is tied to Nigeria's macro cycle: falling rates will pressure a securities-heavy balance sheet, and the naira can wipe out gains for a dollar investor. Buy, sized for country risk.
