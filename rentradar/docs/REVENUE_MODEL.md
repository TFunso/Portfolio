# Revenue Model

## Primary: freemium consumer subscription

- **Free tier**: search, top-25 cheapest rankings, affordability calculator,
  1 saved search, email alerts.
- **RentRadar Plus** ($5-9/mo or $39/yr): unlimited saved searches, SMS +
  push alerts, priority "new listing" notification speed, scam-risk detail
  view, comparison tray export.

## Secondary: B2B leads to landlords/property managers

- **Verified Listing** ($15-30/listing/mo): a property manager pays to have
  their vacancy verified (phone/email confirmed, quality-score boosted) and
  surfaced with a "Verified" badge -- directly monetizes the trust layer the
  anti-scam system already builds.
- **Featured placement** within a category ranking (clearly labeled, never
  reordering the actual cheapest-first ranking -- the product's core promise
  can't be sold around).

## Tertiary: affiliate/referral

- Renters insurance, moving services, and utility setup referrals surfaced
  on the listing-detail and lead-generation ("Call Now" / "Visit Listing")
  flow.

## Data licensing (long-term, opt-in)

- Anonymized/aggregated market-rent trend data (median rent by ZIP/category
  over time, sourced from `PriceHistory`) licensed to researchers, city
  housing departments, or market-analytics firms. Never resells individual
  contact info or PII.

## What RentRadar will not do

- Sell renter contact information or search history to third parties.
- Accept payment to suppress a legitimately cheaper listing in the
  cheapest-first rankings -- monetization competes on top of the ranking,
  never against it. This preserves the product's core trust proposition
  (see the brief's own framing: "help users find the absolute cheapest
  rental accommodations").
